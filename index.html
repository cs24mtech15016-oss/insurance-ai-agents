Domain-Modular Distill-and-Route: Cost-Efficient Agentic AI for Insurance on an Open-Source Stack

A technical report and research proposal on jointly optimizing Model Ops and Cost Ops for insurance workflows.

Author: N. Mandipati. Date: 2026-09-18.

This report states a problem, reviews the existing research and commercial landscape, proposes a concrete system with a named contribution, and lays out an evaluation plan. Papers are cited by arXiv ID. Benchmark figures are author-reported unless stated otherwise; commercial figures are vendor-reported and directional.

Abstract

Insurance work is document-heavy, rule-bound, high-volume, and split between a large routine majority and a small complex tail, with every task tied to a financial KPI. This makes it a good testbed for cost-efficient agentic AI, but the dominant deployment pattern (a single frontier model behind a prompt) is neither the cheapest nor the most auditable option.

This report proposes Domain-Modular Distill-and-Route (DAR): an open-source architecture in which (i) each insurance line of business is a hot-swappable module of a LoRA adapter, a retrieval collection, and a config profile served on a shared multi-adapter backend; (ii) two orthogonal routers select the module and then the model tier; (iii) a distilled small model handles the routine majority and escalates the complex tail; and (iv) the distillation target and the routing threshold are treated as coupled decision variables that jointly minimize cost per resolved task, closed by an escalation-telemetry feedback loop that triggers re-distillation. DAR adds a multi-turn continuation router that escalates when the small model stalls within a case and relaxes back when it converges.

The contribution is threefold. First, a critical synthesis of routing, distillation, context optimization, and serving research, with explicit gaps. Second, a joint Model-Ops/Cost-Ops formulation that co-evolves the small model and the router rather than treating the small model as fixed, together with the operational metrics that expose the coupling (the light-to-remote ratio and cost per resolved task). Third, an evaluation plan with baselines drawn from the routing literature (RouteLLM, FrugalGPT, Hybrid LLM, AutoMix), insurance and document-AI datasets, and pre-registered hypotheses. We ground the economics with a self-host-versus-API break-even and a distill-then-route cost model, and we treat the Indian market (IRDAI, NHCX, DPDP Act, multilingual requirements) as a first-class deployment target where compliance and cost arguments align on self-hosting.

1. Introduction

1.1 Motivation

Four properties of insurance work shape any AI design.

Document density. Every workflow begins with unstructured intake: ACORD forms, loss runs, statements of value, medical bills, police reports, dec pages. Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) is the base capability, not an add-on.

Rule-boundedness. Underwriting guidelines, policy wording, and regulation are explicit rules. This suits retrieval grounding and, more importantly, the citable explanations regulators expect.

Volume with a long tail. Roughly 80–90% of claims and submissions are near-identical routine cases; 10–20% are genuinely complex. This distribution is the structural reason routing pays off.

Dollar-denominated outcomes. Each task maps to a KPI (loss ratio, combined ratio, cycle time, straight-through-processing rate), so the right optimization target is cost per resolved task, not cost per token.

1.2 Problem statement

Given an insurance carrier with a corpus of historical claims, policies, and adjudications, and a set of workflows (claims/FNOL, underwriting intake, fraud, document extraction, servicing), build an agentic system that resolves the routine majority automatically at low marginal cost, escalates the complex tail to higher-capability models or humans, remains auditable and compliant, and scales across many lines of business without linear GPU cost growth. The system must be reproducible on an open-source stack so that a carrier owning its data can internalize the capability rather than rent it.

1.3 Research questions

  • RQ1. Can a distilled domain small language model (SLM) behind a tier router match frontier-level task resolution on insurance workflows at a fraction of cost, and what cost/quality frontier is achievable (measured by APGR and AIQ)?
  • RQ2. How should the distillation quality of the SLM (its first-pass success s) and the routing threshold (τ) be chosen jointly to minimize cost per resolved task, and how does the escalation-penalty coupling behave empirically?
  • RQ3. Does multi-turn continuation routing reduce cost or improve resolution over single-turn routing in agentic claim workflows?
  • RQ4. Does domain-modular multi-adapter serving scale to many lines of business at near-constant GPU cost while preserving per-domain quality?
  • RQ5. For a regulated, multilingual market (India), what is the cost, quality, and latency impact of in-country self-hosting with Indic-language support versus frontier APIs?

1.4 Contributions

  1. A critical field study of the relevant research (routing, PEFT and preference optimization, distillation, context/KV optimization, serving systems, document AI and domain LLMs) and the commercial landscape including India, ending each theme with an explicit gap (Section 3).
  2. The DAR architecture and a joint Model-Ops/Cost-Ops objective that co-evolves the SLM and the router, with the operational metrics (light-to-remote ratio, cost per resolved task) that make the coupling measurable, and a novel multi-turn continuation router (Section 4).
  3. A consolidated technical-foundations reference for the primitives DAR composes (Section 5), an economic model (Section 7), and an evaluation methodology with baselines, metrics, ablations, hypotheses, and threats to validity (Section 6).

1.5 Scope and non-goals

This report covers architecture, algorithms, economics, and an evaluation plan. It does not report a completed empirical study; Section 6 defines the experiments to run. It does not propose new base-model pretraining. Actuarial pricing models (GLM/GAM) are treated as an interface, not a contribution.

2. Background

Insurance fundamentals used throughout are summarized here; Appendix A gives the full primer.

Six principles govern the domain: risk transfer and pooling, insurable interest, utmost good faith, indemnity, subrogation, and contribution/proximate cause. Two lifecycles recur across every line of business with the same shape. The policy lifecycle runs submission, underwriting, quote, bind, issuance (dec page), servicing, renewal, cancellation. The claims lifecycle runs FNOL, triage, investigation and coverage, reserving, adjudication, settlement, subrogation, closure. The economics reduce to loss ratio, expense ratio, and combined ratio (under 100% is an underwriting profit), with reserves split into case and IBNR. Common documents (ACORD forms, dec page, certificate of insurance, loss run, statement of value, FNOL/FROI) recur across lines, which is what makes a shared base model plus per-line adapters natural.

The technical primitives DAR composes (PEFT, distillation, routing, context compression, multi-adapter serving) are defined in Section 5; Section 3 reviews the research behind them.

3. Related Work and Field Study

This section reviews the research the proposal builds on and competes with. Each theme closes with the gap DAR addresses.

3.1 Model routing between LLMs

The core idea is to send each query to the cheapest model likely to answer it well. Two families exist.

Predictive routers decide before generation. RouteLLM (arXiv:2406.18665) learns a binary router R: query→{strong,weak}, routing to the strong model when P(strong wins|q)>α, and provides four instantiations: a similarity-weighted Bradley-Terry model (P(strong wins)=σ(ξ_s−ξ_w), non-parametric, adds under 0.4% cost), matrix factorization, a BERT classifier, and a Llama-3-8B classifier. It defines PGR (performance gap recovered), APGR (PGR integrated over cost), and CPT(x%) (calls needed for a target PGR). On MT-Bench (GPT-4 vs Mixtral-8x7B) matrix factorization reaches APGR 0.802 and up to 3.66× cost reduction at about 95% of GPT-4 quality, and the routers transfer to new model pairs without retraining. Hybrid LLM (arXiv:2404.14618) routes on the quality gap H(x)=Q_small−Q_large with a DeBERTa-v3 router and cuts large-model calls by about 22% at a 1% quality drop.

Cascades decide after generation. FrugalGPT (arXiv:2305.05176) queries models cheapest-first, accepting answer a_i when a DistilBERT scorer g(q,a_i)≥τ_i, solving max E[r] s.t. E[cost]≤b; it reports up to 98.3% cost reduction on HEADLINES at 1.5 points above GPT-4. AutoMix (arXiv:2310.12963) adds self-verification and a POMDP meta-router that classifies queries easy, complex, or unsolvable and solves with an AdaOps particle filter in under 1ms, reporting over 50% cost reduction. RouterBench (arXiv:2403.12031; 405,467 samples, 11 models, 8 datasets) standardizes evaluation with the AIQ metric and finds cheap models are often optimal, GPT-4 is seldom chosen, cost varies 2–5× at equal quality, and cascades beat predictive routers when judge error stays under 0.1 but degrade past 0.2. GraphRouter (arXiv:2410.03834) and RouterDC (arXiv:2409.19886) improve generalization to new models and inductive settings; Aurelio’s semantic-router does embedding-similarity routing in about 10ms.

Gaps. First, this literature treats the small model as fixed: routing decides among given models, but does not co-optimize the small model itself through distillation. Second, almost all of it is single-query; multi-turn agentic workflows, where a case unfolds over several turns, are barely addressed (AutoMix’s POMDP is the closest, but per query). Third, none of it is domain-modular: there is no operational framework for running many specialized models (one per line of business) under a single router with per-domain thresholds. DAR targets all three.

3.2 Parameter-efficient fine-tuning and preference optimization

LoRA (arXiv:2106.09685) learns a low-rank update to frozen weights (10,000× fewer trainable parameters, no added latency once merged). QLoRA (arXiv:2305.14314) back-propagates through a 4-bit base with NF4 and paged optimizers, fine-tuning 65B on one 48GB GPU. DoRA (arXiv:2402.09353) decomposes weight into magnitude and direction and beats LoRA with no inference overhead; PiSSA, rsLoRA, LoRA+, and VeRA refine initialization, scaling, learning rate, and memory. For alignment, DPO (arXiv:2305.18290) replaces RLHF with a closed-form classification loss; ORPO (arXiv:2403.07691) is reference-free and single-stage; KTO (arXiv:2402.01306) needs only binary labels; SimPO (arXiv:2405.14734) is reference-free and length-controlled; GRPO (arXiv:2402.03300) drops the critic for verifiable-reward tasks.

Gap. This work is mature and general-purpose. What is missing for our setting is treating the PEFT-produced specialist as one endpoint of a cost objective: the literature optimizes model quality, not the joint cost of a model-plus-router system. DAR uses these methods but places their output inside the routing cost function.

3.3 Knowledge distillation

MiniLLM (arXiv:2306.08543) uses reverse KL and on-policy training to distill without over-covering the teacher’s low-probability mass. GKD (arXiv:2306.13649) is on-policy with teacher scoring of student generations. DistiLLM (arXiv:2402.03898) adds skew-KL and adaptive off-policy sampling for 4.3× faster distillation. Evol-Instruct/WizardLM (arXiv:2304.12244) generates increasingly complex synthetic instructions. FinGPT (arXiv:2306.06031) is the most relevant demonstration: a domain specialist via LoRA on an open base for under $300, versus roughly $2.67M for BloombergGPT (arXiv:2303.17564).

Gap. Distillation is studied as a one-off model-production step. In a deployed router, the right distillation target and cadence depend on the routing threshold and observed escalation, and drift makes re-distillation a recurring decision. That closed loop is not formalized in the distillation literature; DAR formalizes it (Section 4.5).

3.4 Context and KV-cache optimization

Prompt compression: LLMLingua (arXiv:2310.05736) prunes tokens by small-LM perplexity (5× compression at 79.08 EM on GSM8K vs 78.85 uncompressed); LongLLMLingua (arXiv:2310.06839) is question-aware with contrastive perplexity and document reordering (+21.4% at 4× on NaturalQuestions); LLMLingua-2 (arXiv:2403.12968) casts it as token classification. Caching: SGLang RadixAttention (arXiv:2312.07104) reuses KV pages in a radix tree for up to 6.4× throughput. KV eviction and quantization: H2O (arXiv:2306.14048), StreamingLLM (arXiv:2309.17453), SnapKV (arXiv:2404.14469), PyramidKV (arXiv:2406.02069, matches full cache at 12% retention), KIVI (arXiv:2402.02750), KVQuant (arXiv:2401.18079, under 0.1 PPL at 3-bit). Architecture and RAG: MQA/GQA (arXiv:1911.02150, arXiv:2305.13245), sliding-window attention (Longformer, BigBird, Mistral-7B), and RECOMP (arXiv:2310.04408, 6% compression).

Gap. Compression is evaluated on generic accuracy, not on whether it drops legally material context. In insurance, compressing away a coverage exclusion or an endorsement is a compliance failure, not a small accuracy hit. DAR treats compression as compliance-bounded and includes it as an evaluation axis (Section 6).

3.5 Serving systems

vLLM PagedAttention (arXiv:2309.06180) cuts KV fragmentation to under 4% for 14–24× throughput over HF. Multi-adapter serving is the enabler for domain modularity: S-LoRA (arXiv:2311.03285) serves thousands of adapters at about 4× throughput, and Punica (arXiv:2310.18547) batches adapters against a shared base with its SGMV kernel for 12× throughput at about 2ms/token. Quantization (GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, SqueezeLLM) and speculative decoding (Medusa arXiv:2401.10774, EAGLE arXiv:2401.15077, up to 5.6×) reduce cost and latency; guided decoding (xgrammar arXiv:2411.15100) makes schema-constrained extraction reliable.

Gap. Multi-adapter serving is a systems capability without a domain-management framework on top: how to map lines of business to adapters, route to them, and manage per-domain thresholds and re-distillation. DAR supplies that layer.

3.6 Document AI and domain LLMs

LayoutLMv3 (arXiv:2204.08387; FUNSD F1 92.08, CORD 97.46, DocVQA ANLS 83.37), Donut (arXiv:2111.15664, OCR-free), DocLLM (arXiv:2401.00908, layout-aware, wins 14/16), Docling (arXiv:2408.09869), and Table Transformer (arXiv:2110.00061) cover extraction. InsuranceQA (arXiv:1905.13538) is the main public insurance dataset; FinBERT (arXiv:1908.10063) and FinGPT cover finance. Fraud detection uses graph methods (BiRank arXiv:2009.08313; AutoFraudNet arXiv:2301.07526). Presidio handles PII/PHI de-identification; Model Cards (arXiv:1810.03993) support audit.

Gap. Document AI is strong on extraction benchmarks (FUNSD, CORD, DocVQA) but these are not insurance-native, and there is little public work connecting extraction accuracy to downstream claim-resolution cost. DAR’s evaluation ties IDP quality to end-to-end cost per resolved task.

3.7 Commercial landscape

Global vendors (Shift Technology, Bevaya/Roots Automation, Indico, Cytora, EvolutionIQ, Sprout.ai, Akur8, Tractable, Sensible) sell claims, underwriting, fraud, extraction, and pricing products; their advantage is proprietary insurance data and audit tooling, not model architecture (Section 8.1, Table). In India, the paid B2B products are Perfios (health-claims extraction, underwriting, fraud), Zopper (agentic bancassurance), Riskcovry (recommendation and workflow infrastructure), Signzy (KYC), Mantra Labs (engineering services), and Vymo (sales enablement); B2C is led by aggregators and digital insurers (PolicyBazaar, Acko, Go Digit, Star Health). Two frequently cited Indian health-claims-adjudication vendors (Vitraya, Artivatic/ALFRED) could not be independently verified in this study and are noted without endorsement (Section 8.4).

Gap. The commercial capability exists but is closed and data-moat-driven. No open, reproducible reference architecture exists that a data-owning carrier can build on. DAR is that reference architecture.

3.8 Synthesis

The primitives are mature but studied in isolation and in generic domains. The recurring gaps are: routing treats the small model as fixed; the distillation loop is not closed against routing telemetry; routing is single-turn; there is no domain-modular management layer; compression ignores compliance; and no open insurance reference architecture ties these together with a cost-per-resolved-task objective. The next section proposes a system that closes these gaps.

4. Proposed Solution: Domain-Modular Distill-and-Route (DAR)

4.1 Overview and design principles

DAR serves many insurance lines of business from one shared base model, specializing each line with a small adapter and retrieval collection, and routes traffic so that the routine majority is handled by a cheap distilled SLM while the complex tail escalates. Five principles:

  1. Specialize by module, not by model. A domain is data, not infrastructure.
  2. Route on two orthogonal axes: which domain, then which tier.
  3. Co-evolve the small model and the router against one cost objective.
  4. Make compression and escalation compliance-bounded and auditable.
  5. Prefer self-hosting where economics and regulation agree (notably in India).

4.2 The domain module

A domain (line of business: Auto, Home, Health, Life, Commercial P&C, Workers’ Comp, Cyber) is a registry entry of three parts: a LoRA adapter on the shared base, a retrieval collection, and a config profile (document schema, guardrails, router threshold, escalation model, keywords). All three hot-swap on shared infrastructure, so adding a line adds an adapter, not a GPU.

domains:
  auto:
    adapter: s3://models/adapters/auto-claims-3b-v4
    rag_collection: auto_policies
    doc_schema: schemas/auto_fnol.json
    guardrails: [pii, auto_coverage_check]
    router: { slm_confidence_threshold: 0.82, escalation_model: llama-70b }
    keywords: [collision, vehicle, vin, at-fault]
  workers_comp:
    adapter: s3://models/adapters/wc-claims-3b-v2
    rag_collection: wc_regulations
    doc_schema: schemas/wc_froi.json
    guardrails: [pii, phi, jurisdiction_check]
    router: { slm_confidence_threshold: 0.90, escalation_model: llama-70b }
    keywords: [injury, employer, froi, return-to-work]

Two composition axes exist: line of business and workflow (underwriting, FNOL, servicing, fraud). Compose them with paired keys (auto/claims) or stacked adapters (base + line-LoRA + workflow-LoRA on multi-adapter serving). Start with the line axis; split by workflow only when it diverges enough to earn its own adapter.

4.3 Two-stage routing

A domain classifier answers “which line of business,” using keyword or embedding similarity, or simply the intake channel that already knows, and selects the module. The tier router then decides SLM versus escalation at that module’s threshold τ, using a predictive router (RouteLLM-style matrix factorization or a DeBERTa classifier on the quality gap) for low latency, with a cascade fallback (FrugalGPT-style scorer) when predictive confidence is low. Real-time FNOL uses the predictive path; batch document processing can afford the cascade.

4.4 Continuation routing (multi-turn)

Insurance cases are multi-turn: a claim is triaged, questioned, investigated, and adjudicated over several agent turns. Single-turn routers re-decide each turn independently. DAR adds a continuation router that carries state across turns of one case and decides, per turn, whether to stay on the SLM, escalate, or relax back.

Formulate it as a POMDP ⟨S, A, T, R, Ω, O⟩ over a case. The hidden state S is the case’s true difficulty and resolution progress. Actions A are {stay-on-SLM, escalate-to-frontier, request-human, relax-to-SLM}. Observations O are per-turn signals: SLM confidence, self-consistency across samples, retrieval coverage, tool-call success, and whether the turn advanced the case (phase transition). The reward R = resolution_value − λ·cost penalizes cost and rewards resolution, with a large negative term for an incorrect auto-adjudication (asymmetric, because a wrong claim decision is expensive). The policy escalates when the SLM stalls (confidence and progress drop over consecutive turns) and relaxes back when the case converges (high confidence, stable extraction), so a case can be escalated for one hard sub-question and returned to the SLM afterward rather than pinned to the frontier model for its remainder.

This generalizes AutoMix’s per-query meta-router to a per-case, multi-turn policy. It is the least-established part of the proposal and is stated as an open contribution to be validated in Section 6 (RQ3).

4.5 Joint Model-Ops/Cost-Ops objective and the closed loop

The central claim is that the small model and the router must be optimized together. Let the router send fraction p of traffic to the SLM, which resolves a fraction s of what it attempts on first pass (its distillation quality); the rest escalate. The per-task cost is:

C_task = p·[ C_slm + (1 − s)·C_api ] + (1 − p)·C_api

The (1 − s)·C_api term is the escalation penalty: a cheap SLM with low s is not cheap, because every failure pays for both models. Model Ops raises s (better distillation); Cost Ops raises p and tunes τ (better routing). They are one optimization. The operational efficiency gauge is the light-to-remote (L/R) ratio (Section 5.5), and minimizing C_task at fixed quality is equivalent to maximizing L/R at fixed quality.

The loop is closed by telemetry. A rising per-domain escalation rate (1−s) (equivalently a falling L/R at fixed PGR) signals that the SLM has drifted relative to the traffic, and triggers re-distillation of that domain’s adapter. The cadence is itself a cost decision: re-distill when the drift-induced escalation cost exceeds the amortized training cost C_train/N_life. This makes distillation a recurring, telemetry-driven operation rather than a one-off, which the distillation literature does not model.

4.6 Compliance and multilingual design

Auditability. Every decision carries its retrieved sources, the adapter and model version, and the confidence that drove any escalation, written as a Model Card-style record. Compression is compliance-bounded: legally material spans (coverage, exclusions, endorsements, jurisdiction) are protected from prompt compression and KV eviction, and this is an evaluation axis (Section 6), not an assumption.

Human-in-the-loop as a calibrated gate. The escalation-to-human action fires above a calibrated confidence/value threshold, so the routing threshold and the review threshold are the same object, tuned on expected-cost grounds.

Multilingual (India). A multilingual base (Qwen 2.5) plus Indic resources (AI4Bharat IndicTrans2 and Indic embeddings, and the Bhashini stack) handles regional languages. Language is a third front-door axis alongside domain, with per-language evaluation because quality varies across languages.

4.7 Algorithms

Tier + continuation routing per turn of a case:

state ← case_state            # confidence history, phase, escalation flag
obs   ← observe(turn)         # SLM confidence, self-consistency, retrieval coverage, progress
if not state.escalated:
    if predictive_router(obs) < τ_domain:          # predictive gate
        ans ← SLM(query, adapter_d, rag_d)
        if scorer(query, ans) ≥ τ_accept: return ans        # cascade check
    escalate; state.escalated ← True
if state.escalated:
    ans ← FRONTIER(query, rag_d)
    if confidence(ans) ≥ τ_relax and progress_stable(state):
        state.escalated ← False                    # relax back to SLM next turn
    if confidence(ans) < τ_human or value(case) > V: route_to_human()
    return ans

Telemetry-driven re-distillation trigger per domain d:

observe escalation_rate_d, L/R_d over window W
if escalation_rate_d rising and (drift_escalation_cost_d > C_train_d / N_life_d):
    D_new ← teacher_label(recent_escalated_cases_d)     # teacher generates targets
    adapter_d' ← distill(base, D_new; MiniLLM/GKD) ; align(KTO on adjuster labels)
    quantize(adapter_d', 4bit) ; canary_eval(adapter_d')
    if canary passes: hot_swap(adapter_d ← adapter_d')  # S-LoRA, no redeploy

5. Technical Foundations

This section defines the primitives DAR composes, with the equations and figures behind the design choices.

5.1 Parameter-efficient fine-tuning

LoRA (arXiv:2106.09685) freezes W₀ and learns a low-rank update:

h = W₀x + (α/r)·BAx,   B∈ℝ^{d×r}, A∈ℝ^{r×k}, r≪min(d,k)

B is zero-initialized, A Kaiming-initialized (identity at start), scale α/r (α typically 16–32), target modules q,k,v,o and gate,up,down, trainable parameters about 2·d·r per layer. QLoRA (arXiv:2305.14314) adds NF4, double quantization (~0.4 bit/param saved), and paged optimizers, fine-tuning 65B on one 48GB GPU at 16-bit quality. DoRA, PiSSA, rsLoRA, LoRA+, and VeRA refine it.

5.2 Preference optimization

DPO (arXiv:2305.18290):

ℒ_DPO = −E_{(x,y⁺,y⁻)} log σ( β[ log(π_θ(y⁺|x)/π_ref(y⁺|x)) − log(π_θ(y⁻|x)/π_ref(y⁻|x)) ] )

with implicit reward r(x,y)=β·log(π_θ/π_ref). ORPO folds preference into SFT via an odds-ratio penalty (no reference model); KTO uses binary labels; SimPO is reference-free with a margin; GRPO uses group-relative advantage A_i=(r_i−mean(r_G))/std(r_G) and no critic. For DAR, KTO fits adjuster accept/reject labels naturally.

5.3 Distillation

MiniLLM (arXiv:2306.08543) minimizes reverse KL KL(π_student‖π_teacher) on-policy; GKD (arXiv:2306.13649) scores student generations with the teacher; DistiLLM (arXiv:2402.03898) adds skew-KL for 4.3× faster distillation. The DAR recipe: teacher labels claims decisions and extractions, distill to a 3–7B student (MiniLLM/GKD), align with KTO on adjuster labels, quantize 4-bit, serve on multi-adapter vLLM.

5.4 Serving internals

PagedAttention/vLLM (arXiv:2309.06180): KV fragmentation under 4%, 14–24× over HF. Multi-adapter: S-LoRA (arXiv:2311.03285, thousands of adapters, ~4×), Punica (arXiv:2310.18547, SGMV, 12× at ~2ms/token). Quantization: GPTQ (3.25–4.5×), AWQ (3×+, 4-bit 70B on mobile GPU), SmoothQuant (W8A8), SqueezeLLM. Speculative decoding: Medusa 2.2–3.6×, EAGLE up to 5.6×, distribution-preserving. Guided decoding: xgrammar (arXiv:2411.15100, up to 100× faster constrained generation) forces the ACORD/FNOL schema.

Serving efficiency: the latency–rate (L/R) frontier. Per-request latency is TTFT (prefill, compute-bound) + N_out·TPOT (decode, memory-bandwidth-bound). As request rate R rises, batching lifts throughput and utilization U but also latency L; efficient serving operates near the knee of the L-vs-R curve with goodput (requests/sec meeting SLO) as the KPI. Decode has low arithmetic intensity I = FLOPs/bytes_moved and is memory-bandwidth-bound; batching amortizes weight loads, raising I and U until KV bandwidth/capacity binds. Routing concentrates traffic on the SLM, enlarging batches and raising U, so the router L/R and this serving L/R improve the same cost line. Serve interactive (FNOL, chat) and batch (indexing) traffic on separate queues.

5.5 Routing metrics and the light-to-remote ratio

The literature standardized on quality-at-cost curves: PGR/APGR/CPT (RouteLLM) and AIQ (RouterBench). The operational efficiency gauge is the light-to-remote (L/R) call ratio. With router fraction p to the SLM and first-pass success s:

light calls  = p·N
remote calls = (1−p)·N + p·(1−s)·N = N·(1 − p·s)
L/R = p / (1 − p·s)

Limits: s→1 gives L/R = p/(1−p) (pure gain); s→0 gives L/R → p (the escalation penalty). Blended cost versus all-remote: C_blended/C_remote = (1 − p·s) + (C_light/C_remote)·p. L/R alone is misleading (maximized by sending everything to the SLM and destroying quality), which is why the benchmarks pair cost with a quality metric. Read L/R with APGR; a falling L/R at fixed PGR is the earliest drift signal (Section 4.5).

5.6 Context and KV optimization

LLMLingua (arXiv:2310.05736, 5–20× compression), LongLLMLingua (arXiv:2310.06839, question-aware, contrastive perplexity s_i=ppl(x_i|x_<i)−ppl(x_i|x_que,x_<i)), RadixAttention (arXiv:2312.07104, prefix reuse), H2O/StreamingLLM/SnapKV/PyramidKV (KV eviction; PyramidKV matches full cache at 12% retention), KIVI/KVQuant (2–3 bit KV), GQA/MQA, RECOMP (arXiv:2310.04408). In DAR these are compliance-bounded (Section 4.6): prefix-cache the stable guideline block across claims, compress long medical/legal documents only where material spans are protected.

6. Evaluation Methodology and Expected Results

6.1 Datasets

Public: InsuranceQA (arXiv:1905.13538) for insurance QA and answer selection; FUNSD, CORD, DocVQA, DocLayNet as proxies for form extraction; FinancialPhraseBank/TFNS (via FinGPT) for finance NLP. Proposed carrier corpus: historical claims with adjudicator decisions and extractions, split by line of business, with human labels as ground truth for task resolution. Teacher-generated synthetic data (Evol-Instruct style) for distillation targets, decontaminated against test sets.

6.2 Baselines

Routing: single frontier model; single distilled SLM; RouteLLM (matrix factorization and BERT); FrugalGPT cascade; Hybrid LLM; AutoMix. Serving: vLLM baseline vs S-LoRA vs Punica for multi-adapter throughput. IDP: LayoutLMv3 and Donut for extraction. These baselines let each DAR component be isolated.

6.3 Metrics

Task resolution accuracy against human adjudicators; APGR and CPT(x%) (RouteLLM); AIQ (RouterBench); cost per resolved task (dollars, Section 7); L/R and per-domain escalation rate; calibration (ECE) for the HITL gate; latency (TTFT, TPOT) and goodput per SLA class; extraction F1 for IDP; per-language quality for the Indic layer; and, for compliance, a material-span retention rate under compression.

6.4 Ablations

  1. Distillation co-evolution on/off: fixed SLM vs telemetry-triggered re-distillation, measuring cost per resolved task over time under drift. (b) Single-turn vs continuation routing on multi-turn claim traces (RQ3). (c) Paired keys vs stacked adapters for the line×workflow axes (RQ4). (d) Compression on/off and its effect on material-span retention and resolution (compliance axis). (e) Predictive-only vs predictive+cascade under varying judge reliability (RouterBench shows the crossover near judge error 0.1–0.2).

6.5 Hypotheses and expected results

  • H1 (RQ1): DAR reaches APGR comparable to RouteLLM’s 0.802 on an insurance analogue and 3–10× cost reduction at a 1–2% resolution drop, extrapolating RouteLLM’s 3.66× and FrugalGPT/real-world 5–10× at equal task success. Expected because insurance’s routine/complex split is more separable than open-domain benchmarks.
  • H2 (RQ2): cost per resolved task is minimized at an interior (s, τ) pair, not at extremes; raising s shifts the optimal τ toward more SLM usage (higher L/R) without quality loss.
  • H3 (RQ3): continuation routing reduces cost over single-turn routing on multi-turn traces by relaxing back after transient escalations, with no resolution loss.
  • H4 (RQ4): multi-adapter serving holds per-token cost near-constant as domains grow (Punica-style), with per-domain quality within noise of single-adapter serving.
  • H5 (RQ5): in-country self-hosted DAR meets FNOL latency SLOs and matches frontier resolution on Indic-language claims at materially lower cost, with the compliance benefit of no cross-border data transfer.

6.6 Threats to validity

Judge reliability: RouterBench shows cascades degrade when judge error exceeds 0.2, so the accept/verify scorer must be validated per domain. Teacher hallucination: distillation targets inherit teacher errors, so canary evaluation gates every hot-swap. Distribution shift: the re-distillation loop must detect drift fast enough; detection latency is itself measured. Compression risk: material-span retention is a hard constraint, not an average. Generalizability: vendor-reported commercial figures and author-reported benchmarks may not transfer to a specific carrier corpus, so the proposed corpus study is the primary evidence, with public datasets as reproducible proxies.

7. Economic Analysis

Break-even, self-host versus API: N* = Fixed / (API/req − self/req). A distilled 7B on one A100 (about $2,160/mo) against a $0.01 API call crosses over near 240K requests/month; typical cases fall in 100K–500K. Routine insurance volume sits above it.

Marginal serving cost:

C_per_1M_tok = GPU_$per_hr / (T_tok_per_sec × 3600 × U) × 1e6

At $3/hr, 2,500 tok/s (INT4), U=0.7, this is about $0.48 per 1M output tokens against $15–75 for a frontier API. Utilization U is the largest lever, and routing raises it, so routing saves twice.

Amortized Model-Ops cost: C_modelops_amortized = (C_train + C_teacher_data) / N_life. A high-volume domain amortizes a $2K distillation to about $0.0001/request; a thin domain does not, which is a reason to use RAG rather than a bespoke adapter there.

Distill-then-route (Section 4.5): with p≈0.85, high s, C_slm≈0.1·C_api, blended cost is about 0.235·C_api (~4×); reported real-world gains at equal task success are 5–10×. RouterBench’s finding that GPT-4 is seldom chosen is the empirical signature.

Instrumentation for cost per resolved task: DCGM-exporter to Prometheus for live U and $/hr; register the computed self-hosted $/1M into Langfuse/OpenLIT/LiteLLM custom pricing; keep an amortization ledger in the model registry; tag every trace with domain, tier, escalated, and cache_hit; emit OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions.

8. Deployment: Open-Source Stack, Multi-Domain, and the Indian Market

8.1 Reference stack

Orchestration: LangGraph (stateful graphs), CrewAI (role-based multi-agent), Pydantic AI (typed, durable, OpenTelemetry-native for HITL). Serving: vLLM (PagedAttention, multi-LoRA/S-LoRA, INT4/FP8, speculative and guided decoding); SGLang as an alternative; Ollama/llama.cpp for private inference. Models: Qwen 2.5-14B or Llama 3.x 70B for adjudication; Phi/Gemma/Qwen 1.5–7B as routing/triage SLMs; Donut/LayoutLMv3 for extraction. RAG: Docling/LlamaParse parsing, BGE embeddings + bge-reranker, Qdrant (or Weaviate/pgvector). Guardrails and observability: Guardrails AI, NeMo Guardrails, Qwen3Guard, Presidio; Langfuse (cost), Phoenix/Arize (eval), Ragas/DeepEval.

Global commercial context (vendor-reported): Shift Technology (claims/fraud, top-5 U.S. P&C insurers), Bevaya/Roots Automation (InsurGPT on 300M+ docs), Indico, Cytora (140+ languages), EvolutionIQ (5–10pt combined-ratio reduction), Sprout.ai, Akur8 (pricing), Tractable (auto damage), Sensible (extraction). Their advantage is proprietary data and audit tooling, not architecture, which is why a data-owning carrier can reproduce the capability on this stack.

8.2 End-to-end claims triage

  1. Ingest: Docling/Donut/LayoutLMv3 extract fields; guided decoding forces the schema.
  2. Ground: Qdrant hybrid retrieval (BGE + bge-reranker); prefix-cache the guideline block; compliance-bounded compression for long documents.
  3. Route: domain classifier selects the module; tier + continuation router (Sections 4.3–4.4) send routine cases to the distilled SLM and escalate the rest.
  4. Guard: Presidio, Qwen3Guard, Guardrails AI for PII/PHI, jurisdiction, coverage.
  5. HITL: Pydantic AI durable pause above the calibrated confidence/value threshold.
  6. Observe: Langfuse; report cost per resolved claim, escalation rate, s, cache hit, U.
  7. Close the loop: adjuster corrections become training data; drift triggers re-distillation.

8.3 Multi-domain scaling

Each line of business is a module (Section 4.2) with its own τ and re-distillation cadence: strict for high-exposure lines (Workers’ Comp, Life), looser for high-volume routine (Auto, Travel, Pet). Per-domain tagging gives per-line cost per resolved task and drift. Onboarding a new line (e.g., Cyber): curate data, distill a LoRA, index a collection, write a profile, add one registry block; vLLM hot-loads it.

8.4 The Indian market

India is a distinct market. It is regulated by the IRDAI (goal: “Insurance for All by 2047”), runs on public digital infrastructure (Aadhaar eKYC, DigiLocker, Account Aggregator/DEPA), is standardizing health claims nationally through NHCX (part of the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission), and requires multilingual operation under the DPDP Act 2023 (consent-first, no legitimate-interest basis, consent in scheduled languages, large penalties, conditional cross-border transfer). Bima Sugam is an IRDAI-backed interoperable marketplace.

Paid B2B products (vendor-reported): Perfios (Acclaim health-claims extraction, iAdore underwriting, fraud; customer Bajaj Allianz), Zopper (ZENOVA agentic bancassurance; Bank of Baroda, Axis, IDFC First, ICICI Lombard), Riskcovry (recommendation and workflow infrastructure; 40+ insurers pre-integrated), Signzy (India Stack KYC/Video KYC), Mantra Labs (engineering services; SBI General, Canara HSBC Life), Vymo (sales enablement). B2C is led by PolicyBazaar, Acko, Go Digit, InsuranceDekho, Turtlemint, RenewBuy, and Star Health. Vitraya and Artivatic/ALFRED (health-claims adjudication) are frequently cited but were not independently verified here.

Design implications, all reinforcing DAR: DPDP and health-data sensitivity make self-hosting a compliance argument, aligned with the cost argument; NHCX’s standardized schemas make health-claims adjudication the highest-value first workflow and a natural fit for guided decoding; the language layer uses Qwen plus AI4Bharat (IndicTrans2, Indic embeddings) and Bhashini, with language as a front-door axis; Bima Sugam/NHCX interoperability is an adapter-and-schema concern at the orchestration layer and does not change the Model Ops or Cost Ops design. Government specifications (Bima Sugam, NHCX) should be confirmed against primary IRDAI/NHA sources before implementation.

9. Limitations and Open Questions

The continuation router (Section 4.4) is unvalidated at benchmark scale and is the proposal’s main empirical risk. The re-distillation loop assumes drift is detectable from escalation telemetry before it costs more than re-training; the detection latency is untested. Compression under a hard material-span constraint may limit achievable compression on long legal documents. Vendor and benchmark figures are reported, not reproduced here. The proposed carrier corpus requires data access and human labels that a specific deployment must supply.

Open questions: (1) a closed form for optimal re-distillation cadence under measured drift; (2) auditable single rationale across a split SLM/frontier decision; (3) how fast a router can detect and rebalance for a new claim type (tying GraphRouter’s inductivity to drift); (4) bounds on compression risk for legally material context; (5) whether continuation routing’s gains hold across lines of business with different turn structures.

Appendix A — Insurance Primer

These concepts apply across every line of business and form the shared base or core-adapter layer, trained once so per-line adapters encode only differences.

Six principles: risk transfer and pooling; insurable interest; utmost good faith; indemnity; subrogation; contribution and proximate cause.

Parties: insured, carrier, producer, underwriter, adjuster, actuary, reinsurer, MGA, TPA. Agents must model who holds authority.

Two lifecycles, same shape across lines. Policy: submission, underwriting, quote, bind, issuance (dec page), servicing and endorsements, renewal, cancellation. Claims: FNOL, triage, investigation and coverage, reserving, adjudication, settlement, subrogation, closure.

Money: premium (earned and unearned), deductible/retention/SIR, limit and sub-limit, coverage versus exclusion, coinsurance and copay, reserves (case plus IBNR). KPIs: loss ratio, expense ratio, combined ratio (under 100% is an underwriting profit).

Underwriting: rating factors, rate/rule/form, loss cost, adverse selection, moral hazard, GLM and GAM.

Common documents: application, ACORD forms, dec page, policy wording, endorsements, certificate of insurance, loss run, statement of value, FNOL/FROI.

Regulatory backbone: U.S. state regulation under the NAIC; PII/PHI rules (HIPAA, GDPR); fair and explainable decisioning; anti-fraud; auditability. In India: IRDAI, DPDP Act 2023, NHCX, Bima Sugam.

Shared versus specialized: principles, lifecycle shape, financial vocabulary, common documents, and baseline guardrails are shared; coverage triggers, jurisdiction, medical/legal nuance, and domain forms specialize.
