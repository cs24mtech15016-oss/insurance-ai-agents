Domain-Specialized AI Agents for Insurance on an Open-Source Stack

A Technical Study on Model Ops and Cost Ops — Algorithms, Systems, and Economics

Prepared for: N. Mandipati · Date: 2026-09-18 Method: Synthesis of a market survey and five deep technical literature reviews (routing, context/KV optimization, PEFT/preference/distillation, serving internals, insurance-domain NLP/IDP). Papers are cited by arXiv ID; benchmark numbers are as reported by the source papers and should be read as author-reported unless independently reproduced.

Abstract

Insurance is the strongest commercial proving ground for agentic AI: document-dense, rule-bound, high-volume, with a heavy routine/complex split and dollar-denominated KPIs (loss ratio, combined ratio, cycle time, straight-through-processing rate). This study first surveys the 2025–2026 market of insurance AI agents, then designs a fully open-source stack to reproduce their capabilities, and finally treats the two disciplines the sponsor cares about — Model Ops (training/deploying domain LLMs and SLMs) and Cost Ops (routing, context optimization, cost measurement) — at the level of algorithms, equations, systems internals, and unit economics.

Central thesis. The economically optimal insurance architecture is not a single frontier model but a distilled-SLM + router system. Model Ops produces cheap domain specialists (PEFT + distillation); Cost Ops decides when they are safe to use (predictive/cascade routing) and compresses what they read (prompt/KV optimization). The two couple through one identity — the escalation-penalized cost per resolved task — so they must be optimized jointly. We formalize this coupling (Ch. 8), give a self-host-vs-API break-even and a distill-then-route cost model, and ground every component in the primary literature.

Chapter 1 — Problem Framing

Four structural properties of insurance workflows drive the whole design:

  1. Document density — every workflow starts with unstructured intake (ACORD forms, loss runs, SOVs, medical bills, police reports, dec pages). This makes Intelligent Document Processing (Ch. 7) the foundational capability.
  2. Rule-boundedness — underwriting guidelines, policy wording, and regulation are explicit rules, ideal for RAG grounding and, crucially, for the citable explainability regulators require.
  3. Volume with a long tail — ~80–90% of claims/submissions are near-identical routine; 10–20% are genuinely complex. This distribution is the structural justification for routing (Ch. 5).
  4. Dollar-denominated outcomes — every task has a KPI, so the correct optimization target is cost per resolved task, not cost per token (Ch. 6).

The consequence, developed throughout: route the routine majority to a cheap distilled specialist, escalate the hard minority to a frontier model, and measure the whole thing in resolved-task economics.

2.2 The OSS gap = the opportunity

Vendors’ moat is proprietary insurance data (Bevaya’s 300M docs, Cytora’s pretrained LLMs, Shift’s cross-carrier network) plus explainability/audit machinery — not model architecture. Off-the-shelf OSS orchestration and IDP provide the scaffolding but not the domain knowledge. The reproducible OSS strategy for a carrier that owns its claims/policy corpus: assemble the OSS scaffolding (Ch. 3), specialize via distillation/PEFT on that corpus (Ch. 4), run it economically (Ch. 5–6), and internalize the data moat. All vendor ROI figures above are vendor-reported and directional only.

Chapter 4 — Model Ops: Training Domain LLMs/SLMs (Technical)

4.1 Adaptation decision framework

Need new FACTS?              → RAG (citable, auditable, no retraining)
Need STYLE/FORMAT/behavior?  → Fine-tune
  ├─ <1K examples            → prompt + few-shot + RAG
  ├─ 1K–10K, single task     → SFT with LoRA/QLoRA
  ├─ 10K+, preference        → SFT + DPO/ORPO/KTO/SimPO
  └─ domain corpus (M tokens)→ continued pretraining (DAPT) → SFT
Low budget / on-prem?        → QLoRA (4-bit) or distill to an SLM
Production?                  → quantize (GPTQ/AWQ/FP8) + multi-LoRA serving

4.2 PEFT — the math

LoRA (arXiv:2106.09685). Freeze W₀, learn a low-rank update:

h = W₀x + (α/r)·BAx,   B∈ℝ^{d×r}, A∈ℝ^{r×k}, r≪min(d,k)

B zero-init and A Kaiming-uniform → identity at start. Scaling α/r (α typ. 16–32). Target modules: attention q,k,v,o and MLP gate,up,down. Trainable params ≈ 2·d·r per layer. On GPT-3 175B: 10,000× fewer trainable params, 3× less GPU memory, on-par quality, zero added latency when merged.

QLoRA (arXiv:2305.14314): backprop through a frozen 4-bit base into LoRA adapters via (1) NF4 (information-theoretically optimal for normal weights), (2) double quantization (quantize the quant constants, ~0.4 bit/param saved), (3) paged optimizers. Result: fine-tune 65B on one 48GB GPU (vs ~1,200GB fp16), matching 16-bit quality on Vicuna/MMLU with ~95% less VRAM.

Variants: DoRA (2402.09353) decomposes weight into magnitude+direction, LoRA on direction only — consistently > LoRA, no inference overhead. rsLoRA uses α/√r (stabilizes high rank). LoRA+ uses higher LR on B (~2× faster). PiSSA initializes adapters from SVD principal components (faster convergence, lower quant error). VeRA shares random projections across layers (lower memory).

4.3 Preference optimization — the objectives

DPO (arXiv:2305.18290): reparameterize RLHF to a closed-form classification loss, no reward model or PPO:

ℒ_DPO = −E_{(x,y⁺,y⁻)} log σ( β[ log(π_θ(y⁺|x)/π_ref(y⁺|x)) − log(π_θ(y⁻|x)/π_ref(y⁻|x)) ] )

implicit reward r(x,y)=β·log(π_θ/π_ref); β∈0.1–0.5. ORPO (2403.07691): reference-free, folds preference into SFT via an odds-ratio penalty — no separate ref model or alignment phase; models 125M–7B on UltraFeedback beat some >13B (AlpacaEval2 up to 12.20, MT-Bench 7.32). KTO (2402.01306): prospect-theory “human-aware loss,” needs only binary good/bad labels (not pairs). SimPO (2405.14734): reference-free, average-log-prob reward + target margin — beats DPO by up to +6.4 AlpacaEval2 / +7.5 Arena-Hard. GRPO (DeepSeekMath 2402.03300): critic-free PPO variant, group-relative advantage A_i=(r_i−mean(r_G))/std(r_G) — ~2.7× less memory than PPO; DeepSeekMath-7B hit 51.7% MATH.

Choose When
DPO paired preferences, want simplicity/stability
ORPO want single-stage SFT+align, no ref model
KTO only have binary good/bad labels
SimPO want reference-free + length-controlled
GRPO verifiable-reward tasks (math/code/rules), no critic

4.4 Distillation — teacher → student

  • Sequence-level KD (Kim & Rush): train student on teacher-generated text.
  • MiniLLM (2306.08543): reverse KL KL(π_student‖π_teacher) (avoids the student over-covering teacher’s low-prob mass) + on-policy training on student-generated sequences. 120M–13B: better calibration, lower exposure bias; e.g. 13B→1.5B retains ~85% quality.
  • GKD (2306.13649): on-policy, teacher scores the student’s own generations — fixes train/inference mismatch; integrates with RLHF.
  • DistiLLM (2402.03898): skew-KL loss + adaptive off-policy — 4.3× faster than prior KD at competitive quality.
  • Synthetic data: Evol-Instruct / WizardLM (2304.12244) iteratively evolves instructions to higher complexity; reached >90% of ChatGPT on 17/29 skills.

Insurance instantiation: teacher (frontier or Llama-70B) labels/geneates claims-decision + extraction data → distill to a 3–7B student via MiniLLM/GKD → align with KTO on adjuster accept/reject labels → quantize 4-bit → serve on vLLM multi-LoRA with per-carrier adapters. This reproduces the vendor “insurance-trained model” using your own corpus.

4.5 Continued pretraining & forgetting

DAPT (arXiv:2004.10964): a second LM-objective pretraining phase on in-domain text before task FT; gains in high- and low-resource settings; task-adaptive pretraining helps further. Mitigate catastrophic forgetting with replay (mix general data), LoRA-based CPT (freeze base, adapt in adapters), or EWC.

Chapter 4B — Serving Internals (Technical)

4B.1 PagedAttention / vLLM (arXiv:2309.06180, SOSP 2023)

OS-style paging of the KV cache into fixed-size blocks → fragmentation waste from 60–80% down to <4%, flexible prefix sharing, continuous batching. 14–24× throughput vs HF Transformers, 2.2–3.5× vs TGI; LMSYS production: 5× traffic on 50% fewer GPUs.

4B.2 Multi-LoRA serving — the multi-domain enabler

  • S-LoRA (arXiv:2311.03285): unified paging of adapter weights + KV, heterogeneous-rank batching via custom CUDA (MBGMM/MBGMV), adapters in host memory loaded on demand → serve thousands of adapters, ~4× throughput vs HF PEFT / naive vLLM-LoRA.
  • Punica (arXiv:2310.18547): SGMV kernel batches different adapters against one shared base copy → 12× throughput vs SOTA, +2ms/token.

This is the systems basis for Ch. 9’s per-domain adapters: adding a domain adds an adapter, not a GPU.

4B.4 Speculative decoding (latency)

Draft-verify (Leviathan 2211.17192, 2–3×); Medusa (2401.10774) extra heads + tree attention (2.2–3.6×); EAGLE/2/3 (2401.15077) feature-level autoregression (up to 5.6× on 13B). Distribution-preserving — no quality loss.

Outlines (FSM), xgrammar (2411.15100 — context-independent/dependent token split, up to 100× faster constrained gen, near-zero overhead), lm-format-enforcer. Essential for reliable ACORD/FNOL JSON extraction.

4B.6 Orchestration

SGLang RadixAttention (up to 6.4× vs vLLM/Guidance/LMQL on shared-prefix workloads), KServe (InferenceService, ModelMesh), Ray Serve (fractional GPU, composition), BentoML (packaging/BYOC), emerging llm-d / NVIDIA Dynamo (disaggregated prefill/decode).

4B.7 Serving efficiency: the latency–rate (L/R) frontier

The router L/R of §5.1.1 governs which model runs; the serving L/R governs how efficiently a given model runs on the GPU. Per-request latency splits into two regimes:

Latency = TTFT (prefill, compute-bound) + N_out · TPOT (decode, memory-bandwidth-bound)

The serving-efficiency curve is latency L as a function of request rate R (QPS or concurrency). As R rises, continuous batching raises throughput and utilization U, but per-request L rises too — the two trade off along a frontier with a knee: efficient serving means operating near that knee (high U, L still under SLO), and the right KPI is goodput = requests/sec that meet the SLO, not raw throughput.

Why the curve bends — the roofline ratio. Decode has low arithmetic intensity I = FLOPs / bytes_moved; loading weights + KV per token dominates, so a single stream is memory-bandwidth-bound and wastes compute. Batching amortizes the weight load across requests, raising effective I and U — until KV-cache bandwidth/capacity (or, eventually, compute) becomes the bound. This is the systems reason PagedAttention + continuous batching (4B.1), GQA/MQA and KV quantization (5.2) matter, and it closes the loop with §5.1.1: routing concentrates traffic onto the SLM → larger batches → higher I and U → the router L/R and the serving L/R improve the same cost line together. Chunked prefill (vLLM) tunes the split directly, trading TTFT against TPOT.

Two insurance SLA classes the router must distinguish: interactive (agent turn, chatbot, real-time FNOL triage — TTFT + TPOT bound, keep R below the knee) vs batch (overnight document indexing, bulk loss-run extraction — pure throughput, push R past the knee to maximize U). Serve them on separate queues/replicas or with priority scheduling; report L-vs-R and goodput per class in Langfuse/DCGM.

Chapter 5 — Cost Ops: Routing & Context Optimization (Technical)

5.1 Model routing — algorithms & benchmarks

RouteLLM (arXiv:2406.18665) learns a binary router R: query→{strong,weak} at threshold α (route strong if P(strong wins|q)>α), via four routers: - SW-ranking (Bradley-Terry): non-parametric, cosine-similarity-weighted (γ=10) over Arena battles; P(strong wins)=σ(ξ_s−ξ_w); no gradient training; adds ≤0.4% cost. - Matrix factorization: bilinear win-probability on model/query embeddings (recommender-style). - BERT classifier: CLS + logistic head, full FT. - Causal-LLM classifier: Llama-3-8B predicting label token.

Metrics: PGR (performance-gap recovered), APGR (∫PGR over cost), CPT(x%) (min strong-calls for target PGR). Results (GPT-4 vs Mixtral-8x7B): MT-Bench matrix-factorization APGR 0.802, up to 3.66× cheaper at ~95% GPT-4 quality; MMLU ~20% fewer GPT-4 calls at 50% PGR. Routers generalize across model pairs (→ Claude, Llama-3.1) without retraining.

FrugalGPT (arXiv:2305.05176) — LLM cascade cheapest→dearest, accept a_i when scorer g(q,a_i)≥τ_i (DistilBERT reliability regressor); mixed-integer optimization of max E[r] s.t. E[cost]≤b. Up to 98.3% cost reduction (HEADLINES) at +1.5% over GPT-4.

Hybrid LLM (arXiv:2404.14618) — DeBERTa-v3 router on quality gap H(x)=Q_small−Q_large; deterministic / probabilistic (soft labels from 10 samples) / transformed (relaxed P[H≥−t] for imbalance). ~22% fewer large-model calls at ≤1% quality drop; router adds ~0.036s.

AutoMix (arXiv:2310.12963) — self-verification cascade with a POMDP meta-router ⟨S,A,T,R,Ω,O⟩; verifier confidence (9 levels), reward P−λC, AdaOps particle-filter solver (<1ms lookup). Classifies queries easy/complex/unsolvable (avoid wasteful escalation); >50% cost cut, beats FrugalGPT/HybridLLM; works at 50 training examples.

Benchmarks & meta: RouterBench (2403.12031, 405K samples, 11 models/8 datasets) — metric AIQ (∫ over non-decreasing convex hull); finding: cheap models (Mistral-7B, WizardLM-13B) frequently optimal, GPT-4 seldom chosen, 2–5× cost variation at equal quality; cascading beats predictive when judge error ≤0.1, degrades >0.2. GraphRouter (2410.03834) — heterogeneous GNN edge-prediction, inductive to new LLMs (+9.52% vs C2MAB-V, 99.45% less onboarding time). RouterDC (2409.19886) — dual contrastive (sample-LLM + sample-sample), mDeBERTa 86M, +3.98% in-distribution, ~6× faster than voting. Aurelio semantic-router (MIT) — embedding cosine-similarity routing, ~10ms, Qdrant/Pinecone backed.

Predictive vs cascade tradeoff. Predictive = single forward pass, low latency, but bounded by predictor accuracy and needs calibration to the deployment distribution. Cascade = self-correcting and can beat any single model, but adds sequential/verification latency and degrades fast when the “larger ≥ smaller quality” assumption is violated (~6–13% of queries per FrugalGPT). For insurance: a predictive domain+difficulty router front-door (low latency for real-time FNOL) with a cascade fallback on low confidence.

5.1.1 The light-to-remote (L/R) ratio — the operational efficiency gauge

The literature standardized on quality-at-cost curves (APGR/CPT/AIQ), but the day-to-day operational efficiency gauge of a deployed router is the light-to-remote call ratio: how many queries the cheap/local model absorbs per query that reaches the expensive/remote model. Let a router send fraction p of traffic to the light SLM (first-pass success s); every attempted-local query hits the light model once, and a fraction (1−s) of them escalate. Then over N queries:

light calls   = p·N
remote calls  = (1−p)·N + p·(1−s)·N = N·(1 − p·s)

L/R = light_calls / remote_calls = p / (1 − p·s)

Limits make the coupling explicit: as s→1 (SLM always right) L/R = p/(1−p) — pure routing gain; as s→0 (SLM useless) L/R → p — you pay the light call and still hit remote on everything (the escalation penalty of Ch. 6 surfacing as a collapsed ratio). The effective remote-touch rate is 1 − p·s, and the blended-cost efficiency vs an all-remote baseline is:

C_blended / C_remote = (1 − p·s) + (C_light / C_remote)·p

Critical caveat — L/R alone is a trap. A router maximizes L/R trivially by sending everything to the light model, which is why RouteLLM/RouterBench refused it as a standalone number. L/R is only meaningful read jointly with a quality metric: it is the operational efficiency KPI (drives cost and GPU utilization U), while APGR/CPT/AIQ are the quality-at-cost gauges that keep it honest. Report the pair. In insurance terms, L/R tells you how much of the routine 80–90% the SLM is actually catching; a falling L/R at fixed PGR is the earliest signal of SLM drift and the trigger for re-distillation (Ch. 6). Note that raising s (Model Ops) raises L/R without hurting quality — the two disciplines pull the same lever.

The sponsor’s continuation-routing idea (turn-to-turn escalate-when-stuck / relax-when-converged) is the multi-turn generalization of AutoMix’s meta-router; unpublished at benchmark scale — an open contribution (Ch. 9).

5.2 Context optimization — algorithms & numbers

LLMLingua (arXiv:2310.05736) — small-LM perplexity-based token pruning: budget controller (instructions τ≈0.85, questions τ≈0.9, demos compressed harder) + iterative token-level compression (~100-token segments, per-segment threshold) + distribution alignment. GSM8K 5× compression at 79.08 EM (vs 78.85 full), 20× at 77.33; ~1/25 target-LLM overhead. LongLLMLingua (2310.06839) — question-aware coarse-to-fine: document ranking r_k, contrastive perplexity s_i=ppl(x_i|x_<i)−ppl(x_i|x_que,x_<i) (≈ conditional PMI), document reordering (fights “lost in the middle”), subsequence recovery. +21.4% at ~4× on NaturalQuestions; 94% cost reduction on LooGLE. LLMLingua-2 (2403.12968) — token-classification (XLM-RoBERTa) via GPT-4 data distillation; 3–6× faster than v1.

Prefix/KV caching. vLLM APC (hash-based block reuse). SGLang RadixAttention (2312.07104) — radix tree of KV pages, LRU eviction, longest-shared-prefix-first scheduling (Thm: optimal hit rate when cache≥max req length); up to 6.4× throughput, 3.7× lower latency, production hit rates 50–99%. For insurance, prefix-cache the stable policy/guideline block once, reuse across every claim.

KV compression. H2O (2306.14048) heavy-hitter eviction (submodular) — 20% cache → up to 29× throughput. StreamingLLM (2309.17453) attention sinks (4 initial tokens) + rolling window — 5158→5.40 PPL, 22.2× speedup, 4M tokens. SnapKV (2404.14469) observation-window head-wise selection — 3.6× gen speed, 8.2× memory at 16K, ~380K ctx on one A100. PyramidKV (2406.02069) layer-varying budget (“information funneling”) — matches full cache at 12% retention. Scissorhands (2305.17118) — 5× (20× with 4-bit).

KV quantization. KIVI (2402.02750) 2-bit asymmetric (key per-channel, value per-token) — 2.6× less memory, 2.35–3.47× throughput. KVQuant (2401.18079) pre-RoPE per-channel + non-uniform + dense-and-sparse — <0.1 PPL at 3-bit, 1M ctx on one A100.

RAG compression: RECOMP (2310.04408) extractive+abstractive summarizers with selective augmentation — 6% compression, minimal loss.

Chapter 5B — Cost Measurement With Model Ops In Place

Once you self-host fine-tuned SLMs, API per-token pricing no longer describes cost — it becomes utilization + amortization.

C_request = C_marginal + C_infra_amortized + C_modelops_amortized

Marginal serving cost:

C_per_1M_tok = GPU_$per_hr / (T_tok_per_sec × 3600 × U) × 1e6

Worked (parameterized by Ch. 4B throughput): A100 @ $3/hr, 7B ~2,500 tok/s (INT4, continuous batching), U=0.7 → ≈ $0.48 / 1M output tokens vs $15–75 frontier → 30–150× cheaper. Utilization U is the dominant lever, and routing raises U (concentrates traffic on the SLM) — so routing saves twice.

Amortized Model-Ops cost:

C_modelops_amortized = (C_train + C_teacher_data) / N_life

High-volume domains amortize a $2K distillation to ~$0.0001/req; thin domains don’t (→ use RAG, not a bespoke adapter). Re-distillation cadence is a cost knob, not just quality.

Multi-LoRA allocation (S-LoRA/Punica): base GPU is shared fixed cost allocated by token share; per-domain marginal ≈ SGMV overhead + tiny amortized training. Adding domain N+1 adds no GPU.

Instrumentation: DCGM-exporter→Prometheus for live U and $/hr; register the computed self-hosted $/1M into Langfuse/OpenLIT/LiteLLM custom pricing (traces show real cost, not $0); amortization ledger in the model registry; tag every trace with domain / tier / escalated / cache_hit; emit OTel GenAI semconv so API and self-hosted accounting are identical.

Chapter 6 — The Joint Optimization

Break-even (self-host vs API): N* = Fixed / (API/req − self/req). Example: distilled 7B on one A100 (~$2,160/mo) vs $0.01 API/req → ~240K req/mo crossover; typical 100K–500K. Insurance routine volume sits above it.

Distill-then-route cost function. Router sends fraction p to self-hosted SLM (first-pass success s), 1−p to frontier:

C_task = p·[ C_slm + (1−s)·C_api ] + (1−p)·C_api

The (1−s)·C_api escalation penalty is the crux: a cheap SLM with low success is not cheap — every failure pays both models. Equivalently, this cost is set by the L/R ratio p/(1−p·s) (§5.1.1): the remote-touch rate (1−p·s) is precisely the coefficient on C_api, so maximizing L/R at fixed quality is the same optimization as minimizing C_task. With p≈0.85, s high, C_slm≈0.1·C_api → blended ≈ 0.235·C_api (~4×); reported real-world synergy 5–10× at equal task-success. This is exactly why Model Ops (raise s via distillation, Ch. 4) and Cost Ops (raise p, tune threshold α/τ, Ch. 5) are one problem — and why RouterBench’s finding that “GPT-4 is seldom chosen” is the empirical signature of it.

The closed loop: Cost-Ops telemetry (rising escalation rate = SLM drift) triggers the next Model-Ops re-distillation. Choose re-distillation cadence to minimize C_train/N_life + drift-induced escalation cost.

Chapter 8 — Multi-Domain Design (Onboarding Lines of Business)

A domain (LOB: Auto, Home, Health, Life, Commercial P&C, Workers’ Comp, Cyber…) = { LoRA adapter } + { RAG collection } + { config profile } under one registry key. All three hot-swap on shared infra (S-LoRA base GPU + one Qdrant + one graph), so N domains ≈ the GPUs of one base model.

domains:
  auto:
    adapter: s3://models/adapters/auto-claims-3b-v4    # LoRA on shared base
    rag_collection: auto_policies                        # Qdrant collection
    doc_schema: schemas/auto_fnol.json                   # guided-decoding target
    guardrails: [pii, auto_coverage_check]
    router: { slm_confidence_threshold: 0.82, escalation_model: llama-70b }
    keywords: [collision, vehicle, vin, at-fault]        # domain classifier
  workers_comp:
    adapter: s3://models/adapters/wc-claims-3b-v2
    rag_collection: wc_regulations
    doc_schema: schemas/wc_froi.json
    guardrails: [pii, phi, jurisdiction_check]
    router: { slm_confidence_threshold: 0.90, escalation_model: llama-70b }  # stricter: medical+legal
    keywords: [injury, employer, froi, return-to-work]

Two orthogonal routers: a domain classifier (“which LOB?” — embedding-similarity over keywords, or a lookup since the intake channel usually knows) picks {adapter, collection, profile}; then the tier router (Ch. 5) picks SLM-vs-frontier at that domain’s threshold.

Onboarding a new LOB (e.g. Cyber): curate data → distill a LoRA (hours, ~MB) → index a Qdrant collection → author a profile → add one registry block → vLLM hot-loads it, no redeploy.

Per-domain Cost Ops: each LOB gets its own threshold τ and re-distillation cadence — strict τ for high-exposure lines (WC, Life: PHI/legal), loose τ for high-volume routine (Auto, Travel, Pet). Every trace tagged domain → cost-per-resolved-task, escalation rate, and drift reported per LOB; rising escalation in one triggers that LOB’s re-distillation independently.

Two axes: LOB × workflow (underwriting/FNOL/servicing/fraud). Compose via paired keys (auto/claims) or stacked adapters (base + LOB-LoRA + workflow-LoRA on multi-LoRA). Start with the LOB axis; split by workflow only when it diverges enough to earn a separate adapter.

Chapter 9 — Reference Blueprint & Open Questions

9.1 End-to-end (OSS, claims triage)

  1. Ingest — Docling/Donut/LayoutLMv3 extract fields (guided decoding forces schema).
  2. Ground — Qdrant hybrid retrieval (BGE + bge-reranker); prefix-cache stable guideline context (RadixAttention/APC); optionally RECOMP/LongLLMLingua-compress long medical/legal docs.
  3. Route — domain classifier → per-domain adapter; tier router (RouteLLM/cascade) → distilled 3–7B SLM (vLLM multi-LoRA, INT4) for routine; escalate low-confidence/complex to Llama-70B or frontier.
  4. Guard — Presidio + Qwen3Guard + Guardrails AI (PII/PHI, jurisdiction, coverage).
  5. HITL — Pydantic AI durable pause for adjuster sign-off above a value/uncertainty threshold (= calibrated confidence gate).
  6. Observe — Langfuse traces; report cost-per-resolved-claim, escalation rate, SLM success s, cache-hit, U (DCGM).
  7. Close the loop — adjuster corrections → new distillation/SFT data; escalation-rate rise → re-distill.

9.2 Build vs buy

Buy (Shift/Bevaya/Cytora…) for speed-to-value when you lack a labeled corpus or ML org — you rent their data moat. Build on OSS when you own a large claims/policy corpus and want the moat internalized, on-prem PII/PHI control, and long-run unit economics — FinGPT (<$300 LoRA) shows the specialization cost is small; the recurring cost is data curation and serving, both governed by Ch. 5B.

9.3 Open research questions

  1. Continuation routing for multi-turn claims — POMDP-style turn-to-turn escalate/relax; unpublished at benchmark scale.
  2. Routing under distribution shift — how fast can a router detect distilled-SLM staleness on a new claim type and rebalance τ (ties GraphRouter inductivity to drift)?
  3. Auditable rationale across a split decision — when SLM + frontier both contribute, produce one regulator-grade explanation.
  4. Optimal re-distillation cadence — closed form minimizing training + serving + escalation cost under measured drift.
  5. Compression vs. compliance — bound the risk that 20× prompt compression (LLMLingua) or aggressive KV eviction drops legally material context.

Appendix A — Insurance Domain Primer (shared foundation)

Universal concepts every LOB inherits — architecturally the shared base/core-adapter layer (train once via DAPT/core-adapter), so per-domain adapters encode only deltas.

Six principles: risk transfer & pooling · insurable interest · utmost good faith · indemnity · subrogation · contribution & proximate cause. Parties: insured · carrier · producer · underwriter · adjuster · actuary · reinsurer · MGA · TPA (agents must model authority). Two lifecycles (same shape across LOBs): Policy: submission→underwriting→quote→bind→issuance(dec page)→servicing/endorsements→renewal→cancellation. Claims: FNOL→triage→investigation/coverage→reserving→adjudication→settlement→subrogation→closure. Money: premium (earned/unearned) · deductible/retention/SIR · limit/sub-limit · coverage vs exclusion · coinsurance/copay · reserves (case + IBNR). KPIs: loss ratio, expense ratio, combined ratio (<100% = profit). Underwriting: rating factors · rate/rule/form · loss cost · adverse selection · moral hazard · GLM/GAM. Universal docs: application · ACORD forms · dec page · policy wording · endorsement · COI · loss run · SOV · FNOL/FROI. Regulatory backbone: US state-regulated (NAIC) · PII/PHI (HIPAA/GDPR) · fair/explainable decisioning · anti-fraud · auditability.

Shared vs specialized: principles, lifecycle shape, financial vocabulary, common docs, and baseline guardrails are shared; coverage triggers, jurisdiction, medical/legal nuance, and domain forms specialize.
